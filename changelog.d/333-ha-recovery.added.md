- **Automatic HA in a cluster** (#333, step 4): the leader, with the quorum, restarts the HA VMs of a member whose status is older than 60 s once it is known to be stopped: its BMC powered it off and read it off, or its watchdog, armed when it was last seen, has had its reset delay plus 30 s. The VMs restart one by one, by priority (`high`, `medium`, `low`), on their first preferred node that can take them, else on the online member with the most free memory, never on one in maintenance; a disk still locked by the old node's storage lease is tried again for two minutes; at most 3 automatic restarts of a VM per hour, then it is left to an administrator. With no proof that the member stopped, its VMs are left to an administrator too. Every step is the VM's HA state (the same on every node), an audit entry and a notification. The node that comes back undefines the VMs that moved before anything starts at boot, and HA VMs no longer use libvirt's own autostart. `POST /ha/{vm}/enable` takes `priority` and `preferred_nodes`. The dry-run watcher is now for hosts driven over SSH only.
